Your Password Is Useless-Backdoors in Multiple Sanechips-Based Devices (Part 1)

4G portable Wi-Fi (MiFi) devices built on the Sanechips ZX297520V3 SoC have long been popular for their relatively high performance and low price. However, if your device was not manufactured by ZTE itself but by one of the vendors below, your admin login password is meaningless whether or not you have ever changed it. On some devices, an attacker can even execute arbitrary commands.

1. ALK/QRZL devices

Device description

The manufacturers of these devices are complicated. Most devices and their IMEIs identify themselves as ALK (深圳市阿乐卡科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Aleka Technology Co., Ltd.), yet the model names and manufacturers printed on them vary widely, for example: UG902 (东莞市瑞联祥通科技有限公司 / Dongguan Ruilian Xiangtong Technology Co., Ltd.), MZ391 (Shenzhen Aleka Technology Co., Ltd.), D623+ (深圳市信丰智联科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Xinfeng Zhilian Technology Co., Ltd.), SCM02 (深圳市世创信科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Shichuang Xin Technology Co., Ltd.). There are many such models and they are difficult to enumerate.

It is said that the hardware design house for these devices is 易联科技（深圳）有限公司 / eLink Technology (Shenzhen) Co., Limited, while the largest purchaser and the software solution provider for many later devices is 深圳市启瑞智联科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Qirui Zhilian Technology Co., Ltd. We divide these devices into two categories: devices that enumerate to a PC as “ALK,Incorporated ALK Mobile Boardband” are classified as ALK devices; devices that enumerate as “DEMO,Incorporated DEMO Mobile Boardband” are classified as QRZL devices.

Backdoor details

ALK devices expose a set of ALK_-prefixed debug commands that require no authentication.

The ALK_LOGIN endpoint rewrites the login state to “logged in” without authentication, and ALK_EXC_SYSTEM_CMD executes arbitrary shell commands. Because these Sanechips devices use a single-user architecture, such commands run with root privileges. To prevent users from disabling the cloud-control mechanism, QRZL devices remove most of the debug commands but still retain ALK_LOGIN, presumably for factory debugging.

Simple PoC

ALK_LOGIN: http://<device-admin-panel>/reqproc/proc_post?goformId=ALK_LOGIN
If the admin panel becomes logged in after the request, the device is affected.

ALK_EXC_SYSTEM_CMD: http://<device-admin-panel>/reqproc/proc_post?goformId=ALK_EXC_SYSTEM_CMD&SYS_CMD=reboot
If the device reboots after the request, it is affected.

Affected board numbers

Including but not limited to various ALK/QRZL devices with board numbers AM206, AM231, AM300, AP100, M603, M605, MZ801, MZ803, MZ804, MZ805, MZ901, LKS-L007, LKS-L008, DZ801, DZ803, DZ805, UZ901, MF782T, MF783.
