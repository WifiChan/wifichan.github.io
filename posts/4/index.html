Your Password Is Useless-Backdoors in Multiple Sanechips-Based Devices (Part 2)

2. SZXF devices

These devices are produced by 深圳市信丰伟业科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Signalinks technology Co., Ltd. and are sold both domestically and overseas. Their products include the MF761W, F231ZC and M23L, which are regarded as the “ceiling” (top tier) of the portable Wi-Fi market. Most units are manufactured by Shenzhen Signalinks technology Co., Ltd. under the D623 or MF782 model names; a few customized variants are not.

Backdoor details

In the goahead password verification function sub_FC9C, the supplied password is first compared with xfnj1234 before the real verification takes place.

The same logic is present in sub_13FE4.

Simple PoC

Simply enter xfnj1234 in the admin login password field. For a more advanced approach:
http://<device-admin-panel>/goform/goform_set_cmd_process?isTest=false&goformId=LOGIN&password=eGZuajEyMzQ%3D

Affected models

Mainly the MF782 and D623. Other models based on Signalinks solution are likely affected as well, including the various variants of the F231 and F232, as well as the MF782, MF761, M23L, M24L, M900 and U090.

3. LC devices

LC devices cover many brands, such as KuWfi and Wantang (万唐), plus many unbranded ones. Model numbers may be LM321, LC112, GW522, etc. Most units encountered are CPE devices, but there are also MiFi/UFi products.
The manufacturer may be 深圳市米饭儿数据科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Mifidata Technology Co., Ltd. (https://www.mifidata.com/).

Reverse-engineering target information

Device reverse-engineered: unbranded, board number LC113_UFI_V1.1
Firmware version: LC213_V6.0_V3TP42NOR_B40_EN_20260328
Verification device 1: Wantang GW522 (深圳市米饭儿数据科技有限公司 / Shenzhen Mifidata Technology Co., Ltd.)
Firmware version: LC213_V1.0_V3TP42NOR_B40_EN_20251018
Verification device 2: unbranded, board number LC113_UFI_V1.1
Firmware version: LC113_V4.1_V3TP42NOR_SH_SUPER_20240803

Backdoor details

In the LOGIN handler sub_13D04, after the password passes the length and failure-count checks in sub_13C18, it is first passed to sub_10BCC.

sub_10BCC checks whether the password equals the last four digits of the IMEI.

If it matches, the password is treated as correct and login succeeds directly.

During verification, this feature appears to have been intended by the vendor for entering a hidden eSIM switching page. The problem is that this check is purely client-side; therefore, after logging in with the last four digits of the IMEI, the actual privilege level is admin — the same as visiting the main page directly. This is even more outrageous than Tenda’s rzadmin, so to speak.
Most critically, the IMEI retrieval endpoint is unauthenticated (it is on the whitelist) — the IMEI is even displayed on the home page.

Simple PoC

Just read the IMEI on the home page and enter it in the login box, then log in and re-enter the panel.
For a more advanced approach:
http://<device-admin-panel>/reqproc/proc_get?isTest=false&cmd=imei
then
http://<device-admin-panel>/reqproc/proc_post?isTest=false&goformId=LOGIN&password=<base64 of the last four IMEI digits>

Affected board numbers

Including but not limited to various LC devices such as the LC113 and LC213.

Proof

Summary

Perhaps because MiFi/CPE admin panels are inherently unlikely to be reachable from the outside, these vendors did not remove these backdoors from production devices. Users are advised to use at least WPA2 encryption together with a strong password, to prevent their devices from being exploited by attackers.
